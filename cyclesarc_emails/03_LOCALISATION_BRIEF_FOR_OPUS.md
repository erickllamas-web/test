# Localisation brief — the standing instruction for Opus

Erick's ruling: *localisation is more than substituting the name.* This brief
is what Opus reads before it localises any email, every time. The master
emails are the **meaning**; the localised email is **what a good salesperson
born in that market would write to that person, carrying exactly that
meaning and nothing more.**

Opus does not write new emails. It does not add claims, examples, prices or
flattery. It changes everything that a native reader would notice as foreign,
and nothing that Cycles Arc has ruled.

## 1. What changes (the twelve layers)

| # | Layer | What Opus does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Language** | Writes in the recipient's working language. Not a translation: a rewrite that keeps the four moves (their thing, what they get, one fact, one ask). |
| 2 | **Register** | First contact is formal everywhere: Sie, vous, usted/você (formal), lei, 敬語 (です・ます + 謙譲語), 존댓말 (-습니다). Exceptions where formal reads stiff and *informal is the business norm*: Swedish, Norwegian, Danish, Dutch (du / je), and Brazilian Portuguese (você). Never tú on a first email in Spain or Mexico. |
| 3 | **Address** | Name order and honorific as the market uses them: `Sehr geehrte Frau Dr. Weber`, `Madame Lacascade`, `田中様` (surname + 様), `김민수 님` / `김 상무님` (title where known), `Estimada Sra. Ruiz`, `Gentile Dott.ssa Rossi`. English markets: first name after "Dear". Where the title is unknown, the safe form of that language, never a guess at gender. |
| 4 | **Greeting and sign-off** | The conventional pair, nothing more: `Mit freundlichen Grüßen`, `Cordialement` (first email) / `Bien à vous`, `Cordiali saluti`, `何卒よろしくお願い申し上げます`, `감사합니다`, `Atentamente`, `Med vänliga hälsningar`, `Met vriendelijke groet`. Japanese adds the opening line `お世話になります。` only if there is a prior relation; for a first email `突然のご連絡失礼いたします。` |
| 5 | **Subject line** | The market's convention: Japanese subjects open with 【Cycles Arc】and name the matter; German subjects are nouns; French and Spanish stay sentence-case and short; Korean subjects name the company and the matter `[Cycles Arc] 귀사 자외선 차단제 용기 관련 제안`. |
| 6 | **Dates, numbers, units** | 14 July 2026 / 14. Juli 2026 / 14 juillet 2026 / 2026年7月14日 / 2026년 7월 14일. BC/AD → v. Chr. / av. J.-C. / 紀元前 / 기원전 / a. C. Degrees, minutes, metres stay metric; en-US keeps metric too (the data is metric; say so once). Decimal comma where the market uses it. |
| 7 | **Send window and calendar** | Local working hours, Tuesday to Thursday, 9–11 local. Never a public holiday of that country or region; never the week of a national holiday where the market stops (Golden Week, Chuseok, Lunar New Year, Ramadan for Gulf and Indonesia, August in France and Italy, the week between Christmas and New Year everywhere). Sun-care timing by hemisphere (funnel §2). |
| 8 | **Local vocabulary of their trade** | The word their trade uses: *Planetarium* / *Fulldome*, *production exécutive*, *Lichtgestaltung*, *VFX スーパーバイザー*, *제품 패키징*, *maison d'édition*, *éphéméride*, *Almanach*. The glossary (§4) fixes the Cycles Arc nouns; the trade nouns follow the market. |
| 9 | **The legal notice** | The first-email notice rewritten for the jurisdiction (§6). This is the one place the *content* changes, because the law does. |
| 10 | **Cultural reading of the hook** | The hook stays about *their* project, which is already local. What Opus checks: that the example lands (Titanic is known everywhere; "the Observer" is British; swap a British example for the market's own equivalent only if the master marks it `{{swappable}}`). |
| 11 | **Length** | Japanese and Korean business emails run longer by convention (the opening courtesy, the closing courtesy); German runs slightly longer than English; Spanish and Italian run shorter than people expect. The word limits in the voice sheet become *the equivalent in that language*: the English count × 1.3 for ja/ko, × 1.1 for de, × 1.0 for the rest. |
| 12 | **Spelling and variety** | en-GB for the United Kingdom, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand, India, Singapore, the Gulf; en-US for the United States and Canada (English). fr-FR vs fr-CA (Canada: *courriel*, no anglicisms, the CASL notice). pt-BR vs pt-PT. es-ES vs es-MX/es-419 (*computadora* vs *ordenador*; *usted* in both). de-DE vs de-AT vs de-CH (ß → ss in Switzerland; *Grüezi* never in writing). zh-Hans for the mainland, zh-Hant for Hong Kong and Taiwan; English is acceptable to Hong Kong. |

## 2. What never changes

- **Cycles Arc** is never translated, transliterated or declined. In Japanese
  and Korean, the Latin name stays; the first mention may add the reading in
  parentheses once: `Cycles Arc（サイクルズ・アーク）`, `Cycles Arc(사이클스 아크)`.
- The four moves and their order. The single ask. The word limit (scaled).
- Every fact and every number. Nothing added, nothing dropped, nothing
  rounded. "Within a tenth of a degree" stays a tenth of a degree.
- The seven lint rules (§7), in every language.
- No prices, no astrology words, no other brand, no engine talk, no customer
  names, no "100 %".
- The signature. It is added by the system in the sender's own form; Opus
  never writes a signature.
- The link targets. Link text is localised; the URL is not.
- The sender's name and role. A Japanese email from Nicole Falla still says
  Nicole Falla, with `ニコール・ファラ` after it once if the reader needs it.

## 5. The send window (Opus returns it with every email)

`send_window_local`: the next Tuesday–Thursday 09:00–11:00 in the recipient's
time zone that is not a public holiday there, not inside a market stop (§1
row 7), and not inside the sun-care off-season for Line 2. Opus names the
holiday calendar it used.

## 6. The first-email notice, per jurisdiction

The master notice (voice sheet §6) is the content. The jurisdiction sets the
words. Opus writes the notice in the email's language. **Counsel confirms
each one once; until then they are DRAFT.**

| Regime | Basis | What the notice must say |
|---|---|---|
| EU / EEA / UK (GDPR, UK GDPR, ePrivacy) | Legitimate interest, business to business, work address published | Where we found the address; that we write B2B about a matter related to their role; the right to object; unsubscribe. The frame carries the company name and address. |
| United States (CAN-SPAM) | Opt-out | The frame's physical address; a working unsubscribe honoured within 10 days; an honest subject line; "a business email from Cycles Arc" stays. |
| Canada (CASL) | Implied consent: address conspicuously published, no statement refusing commercial email, message relevant to their role | Name of sender and of Kairoi Co. OÜ; a contact address; unsubscribe; **and the email must be relevant to the role on the card**. Opus adds one line: "We write because this concerns your work as {{role}}." |
| Australia (Spam Act 2003) | Inferred consent for conspicuously published business addresses, relevant to the role | Sender identity; unsubscribe; relevance line as Canada. |
| Japan (特定電子メール法) | Opt-in, with an exception for addresses published for business contact | One line in Japanese: the address was published for business contact at {{host}}; the sender's name, address and the opt-out route. **Only addresses published as business contact points.** |
| South Korea (정보통신망법) | Prior consent for commercial email, narrow exceptions | **Held for Erick's ruling.** Default: no cold email to Korea; use the published business enquiry form instead, and the email text becomes the form text. |
| China (PIPL), mainland | Consent-based | Prefer the published form. Where email: the notice in Chinese, the source named, opt-out. Held for Erick's ruling as Korea. |
| Brazil (LGPD) | Legitimate interest | As EU, in Portuguese. |
| Mexico (LFPDPPP) | Aviso de privacidad | One line pointing to the privacy policy (cyclesarc.com/privacy) in Spanish. |
| Gulf, India, Israel | Lighter regimes | The EU notice in English stands. |

## 8. The prompt (what Opus is given, and what it returns)

### System

> You are the localiser for Cycles Arc, a brand of Kairoi Co. OÜ. You rewrite
> one English master email for one named recipient in one market so that it
> reads as if written by a salesperson from that market, carrying exactly the
> master's meaning. You add nothing, you drop nothing, you round nothing. You
> follow `02_VOICE_AND_RULES.md` and `03_LOCALISATION_BRIEF_FOR_OPUS.md` in
> full. You never write prices, astrology words, other brand names, engine or
> method talk, customer names, or "100 %". You never write a signature. You
> return JSON only.

### User (per email)

```json
{
  "master_id": "L1-E1",
  "master": { "subject": "...", "body": "...", "notice": "..." },
  "recipient": { "name": "...", "name_native": "...", "title": "...", "organisation": "...",
                 "country": "DE", "language": "de", "timezone": "Europe/Berlin",
                 "role_on_card": "Head of Production", "source_host": "example.de" },
  "lead": { "project": "...", "site": "...", "date_or_era": "...", "renderer": null,
            "hook_sentence_en": "..." },
  "sender": { "name": "Nicole Falla", "role": "Business Development Manager" },
  "line": 1,
  "glossary": { "...": "..." },
  "market_row": { "...": "..." }
}
```

### Output (JSON only)

```json
{
  "language": "de-DE",
  "subject": "...",
  "greeting": "...",
  "paragraphs": ["...", "...", "..."],
  "ask": "...",
  "signoff": "...",
  "notice": "...",
  "send_window_local": { "from": "2026-10-20T09:00+02:00", "to": "2026-10-20T11:00+02:00",
                         "calendar": "DE federal + Bavaria" },
  "back_translation_en": "...",
  "word_count": 0,
  "checks": {
    "four_moves_present": true, "single_ask": true, "no_new_facts": true,
    "numbers_identical": true, "lint_CA1_CA7_clean": true, "register": "Sie",
    "address_form": "Sehr geehrte Frau Dr. Weber", "within_length": true
  },
  "changes_made": ["...", "..."],
  "questions_for_reviewer": []
}
```

`changes_made` lists every departure from a literal translation and why
(register, address, subject convention, date form, legal notice, idiom). That
list is what the human reviewer reads first. `questions_for_reviewer` is for
anything the card does not settle (the person's gender for a gendered
greeting, an unknown title, a market not in §3). An email with an open
question is not sent.

### The reviewer's pass (a person, every time)

1. Read `changes_made`. Each change must be one of the twelve layers.
2. Read `back_translation_en` against the master. Same facts, same numbers,
   same single ask.
3. The lint in the target language passes.
4. For a new language, or the first three emails to a market, a native reader
   reads the email itself.
5. Mark ready. Erick approves the batch.
